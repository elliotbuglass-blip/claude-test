# What this is for

## The person

Elliot Cameron (@elliotcameron._) — Newcastle-based content creator and
part-time model working across menswear, automotive and lifestyle. Business
analyst at a law firm by day, which he keeps separate from this. Not
agency-signed, so he runs his own outreach.

What he is going for: PR packages and gifting, paid creator work, and above
all access to cars for automotive content.

## The job this page does

Two things sit in different places and he needs them together:

1. **His Instagram numbers.** He quotes these in outreach emails constantly.
   They need to be correct, current, and phrased the way he would say them
   out loud.
2. **His outreach pipeline.** Threads with brands, press offices and
   dealerships. The failure mode is a thread going quiet and him not noticing
   for three weeks.

The follow-up drafting exists because the gap between "I should chase that"
and "I have written the chase" is where threads die.

## Non-negotiables

**Private.** The Inbox and Follow-ups tabs show real email. This page is never
sent to a brand. A brand-facing version would be a separate build containing
reach, growth, top content and audience only — no email data of any kind.

**Never invent a number.** Every figure on the page traces to a Windsor reading.
If data is missing the page says so rather than estimating. This matters more
than it sounds: numbers from this page end up in emails to press offices, and
an overclaim is worse than no claim.

**Lead with views and reach, not follower count.** His reach runs far ahead of
his follower number and that is the thing worth showing. Follower count is
context, not the headline.

**Plain English everywhere.** Copy on the page, and copy the page generates,
reads like something he would actually say. No marketing language, nothing
that sounds like a pitch deck. If a line sounds written, cut it.

## Tone of the generated drafts

The follow-up drafter has a style prompt baked in (`STYLE` in the source). Its
rules, in order of importance:

- Plain, natural English. No marketing language.
- Three or four sentences at most.
- Never apologise for following up and never grovel. He is not asking for a favour.
- Never invent facts, figures, dates or commitments not in the thread.
- Do not restate the thread back at them.
- Give them an easy reply, or one specific small next step.
- Sign off "Cheers," then "Elliot".
- Body only — no subject line, no preamble.

If you change the drafting prompt, keep every one of those. They were arrived
at by watching drafts fail.
